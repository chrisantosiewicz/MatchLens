**Short description**

MatchLens helps football viewers investigate the actions behind a match moment. Select a player, follow a recorded sequence, compare contributions and inspect the evidence at your chosen match clock. Deterministic statistics support grounded explanations, personalized exploration and English/Spanish recaps. An optional Microsoft Foundry analyst and viewing guide select supported interpretations and available next investigations. A useful local template experience remains available without cloud access. All matches, players, season profiles and positional snapshots are synthetic.

**Project description**

Football intelligence should help a viewer understand what a statistic leaves out. MatchLens connects player statistics to recorded match sequences and lets the viewer investigate at their own replay clock.

In the demonstration, Mateo Silva has no shots, but his lateral pass at 69:07 appears in the recorded build-up to Nico Vega's saved attempt at 69:22. Opening the explanation reveals the four supporting events. This shows a contribution that a progressive-pass total can miss, without claiming that the pass created space or caused the attempt.

A separate corner scenario supplies complete, explicitly synthetic position snapshots for all 22 active players. Viewers compare two attackers in the penalty area at setup with five at delivery, then inspect first contact and outcome. These snapshots support occupancy and endpoint movement; they do not establish intended targets, continuous trajectories or defensive reactions.

The backend computes statistics and eligible claims deterministically. Supporting event IDs, revisions and selected-clock scope remain inspectable. Corrections withdraw dependent explanations. English and Spanish answers and recaps use the same evidence standard. Template, model and fallback outputs are labeled distinctly.

Microsoft Foundry's optional analyst selects canonical interpretations, while a separate viewing guide ranks available server-authored investigations for attacking, defending or circulation interests. Actual authenticated executions, inputs, selections, usage and failures are retained for inspection. A fair comparison did not establish a three-agent advantage, so the preferred controlled workflow uses the simpler selector. The public application preserves useful template operation without paid anonymous model calls.

All football data is synthetic. Independent viewer benefit and incremental model usefulness remain unmeasured. The video distinguishes current local functionality from historical recorded Foundry execution.

**Azure contribution**

MatchLens uses Microsoft Foundry with `gpt-4.1-mini` to select evidence-grounded football interpretations and next investigations, with authenticated execution recorded through its backend Azure integration; Azure App Service hosts the public template demo.
